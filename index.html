1850–1900

Colonial Industrial Period

+

British colonial rule restructured South Asian craft economies to favor imported manufactured goods, devastating traditional handloom industries. Meanwhile, grand colonial architecture reshaped Indian cities, and Indian craft objects were displayed at international exhibitions as exotic spectacle rather than serious design.

colored lithograph

Indian Court, Great Exhibition, London, 1851

The first exhibition of this scale and concept; the first time the general British audiences encountered Indian craft. The Indian Court was the "gem" of the exhibition.

OBJECT / ARTIFACT

Bracelet, displayed at The Great Exhibition, 1851

Rajasthani gold bracelet inset with diamonds and translucent blue enamel, displayed at the Great Exhibition, London, 1851.

1900–1930

Arts & Crafts Revival / Swadeshi

+

Leading up to India's independence, nationalist resistance found a visual form in the Swadeshi movement at this time. It was a call to boycott British goods and revive Indian handcraft. At the same time, the Bengal School of Art rejected Western academic traditions, searching for a distinctly Indian visual language rooted in Mughal miniature and Ajanta fresco.

PAINTING

Abanindranath Tagore — "Bharat Mata," 1905

Constructed a visual identity for India at a moment when defining what the nation looked like was itself a political act. The four arms depicted, holding icons of learning, food, clothing, and liberation were a symbol and promise to a healthy national future.

Object / Symbol

Gandhi's Charkha (Spinning Wheel)

Ghandi reframed the chakra, standardizing it and making it portable. It was a symbol that made the act of spinning homespun cloth into a statement of economic resistance and national self-sufficiency. It appeared on the original Indian National Congress flag up until 1947.

1930–1950

Late Colonial Modernism & Independence

+

Independence created competing visions of what the new nation should look like. Lutyens' New Delhi represented imperial imposition (European classicism with Indian motifs). After 1947, Nehru's government embraced international modernism as a symbol of progress.

Architecture

Rashtrapati Bhavan — Edwin Lutyens, 1929

The imperial presidential palace in New Delhi, European classicism with Indian chhatris (elevated dome pavilions) and other motifs applied as decoration, a visual summary of how colonialism understood Indian design.

Architecture

Chandigarh Capitol Complex — Le Corbusier, 1952

A city commissioned by Nehru as a symbol of modern, independent India, built by a Swiss-French architect who had never visited before winning the commission. Brutalist design values.

1950–1970

National Identity & Design Education

+

A newly independent India needed designers and a visual identity; there was a significant development of a distinct Indian graphic identity (stamps, currency, logos). The Eames India Report (1958) shaped what design education here could look like, leading directly to the founding of the National Institute of Design in Ahmedabad in 1961.

Document

The India Report — Charles & Ray Eames, 1958

A document commissioned by the Indian government that used a simple brass water vessel, the lota, as a case study in perfect design.

Institution

National Institute of Design (NID), Ahmedabad — 1961

IIndia's most influential design school, shaped directly by the Eames report and situated in Ahmedabad alongside the modernist buildings of Corbusier and Kahn.

Ongoing

Vernacular & Traditional Design Systems

+

Running parallel to every other movement are South Asia's living traditional design systems; folk art-esque visual languages. Kolam patterns, encoding mathematical sequences, and Ajrakh block printing using precise geometric resist-dyeing- these traditions predate colonialism and continue today, informing contemporary designers.

PHOTOGRAPH, Street in an agraharam, Tamil-Nadu, 1982

Kolam / Rangoli

Geometric floor patterns drawn daily by hand using rice flour, encoding mathematical sequences and symmetry systems that predate any formal design education.

PRINT-MAKING / TEXTILE

Ajrakh Block Printing— Kutch, Gujarat

One of South Asia's oldest continuous textile traditions, using hand-carved wooden blocks and natural dyes to produce precise geometric patterns unchanged in structure for centuries.

1960–1985

Postcolonial Regionalism

+

Postcolonial regionalism emerged as architects trained under European modernists (Corbusier and Kahn) began developing a distinctly South Asian design language. Rather than importing Western solutions wholesale, architects like like Balkrishna Doshi and Charles Correa drew on local climate, material, and spatial tradition- producing work that engaged seriously with how people lived rather than how modernism imagined they should.

Architecture

Karunaratne House, Kandy, Minnette de Silva, Sri Lanka, 1962

Designed by the first Asian woman member of the RIBA, the house synthesizes Kandyan spatial tradition with modernist structure, making de Silva one of the earliest and most overlooked pioneers of postcolonial architecture in Asia.

Architecture

B.V. Doshi — Aranya Housing, Indore, 1989

Pritzker Prize 2018. An incremental housing project designed to be completed by its residents over time, treating incompleteness as a feature rather than a flaw.

1970–1995

Graphic Design & Bollywood Visual Culture

+

This era produced some of South Asia's most distinctive visual culture. Hand-painted Bollywood film posters, made by trained studio artists in Mumbai, developed into a rich graphic tradition before digital printing made them obsolete in the 1990s. Brand identities like the Air India Maharaja became iconic across Asia, and India's advertising industry grew rapidly out of Mumbai.

Visual Culture

Hand-painted poster for Ram Balram, 1980

One of the many hand-painted Bollywood movie posters of this time. Made in Mumbai, artists spent weeks on a single image, a graphic tradition that vanished almost entirely when digital printing arrived in the 1990s.

Brand Identity

Air India Maharaja — Bobby Kooka & Umesh Rao

A turbaned maharaja mascot, made in 1946 but became iconic through decades of advertising campaigns that ran through the 70s–90s.

1991–2005

Economic Liberalization & Globalization

+

India's 1991 economic liberalization opened markets and transformed consumer culture. There was a new middle class, new retail environments, and new brand identities. Traditional crafts were repackaged for this new consumer- Fabindia being a significant brand that evolved at this time (shifted its focus from exports to the domestic Indian market). Meanwhile the IT boom made Bangalore a new center for software and UX design.

Retail / Brand

Fabindia — Rebrand & Retail Expansion, 1990s

Traditional Indian handloom and craft repackaged through branding for a liberalization-era middle class consumer.

Architecture

Infosys Campus, Bangalore — 1990s

IT corporate campus as a new architectural typology; signaled India's arrival in global tech.

2000–Now

Contemporary Design

+

Contemporary Indian design resists a single definition. Designers working today navigate between decolonial critique and global markets, vernacular craft traditions and speculative futures, local artisan networks and international audiences. Contemporary south asian visual culture emerges into something new and uniquely crafted, changing and deriving from so many different influences.

PUBLISHER

Blaft Publications, Chennai, founded 2007

Independent press reviving Tamil and Telugu pulp fiction with vintage-inspired designed covers, treating vernacular popular culture as worthy of serious graphic design attention.

jewellery / Brand

RBhavya Ramesh, founded Mumbai, 2018

Avant-garde silver jewellery inspired by Indian mythology and craft tradition, made by Jaipur artisans, reframing what Indian jewellery design can look like for a global audience. Also important as such desi designs are readily available for cheap, with cheap low-quality materials in Indian street markets, but this brand makes them higher-quality, long lasting, yet still affordable for their quality, and with more radical design experimentation.
